Your AI campus is on the waitlist. Check its Grid PNR.

RAC doesn't build wires faster; it lets more megawatts use the wires that exist. The building flexes every day, the GPUs ease only when the grid calls, and an independent verifier proves it. In return a campus could connect now and move to firm power when new lines arrive. The utility always decides.

02

Sort the load: the building flexes daily, the GPUs only on a grid call

Layer 1 is the building: pre-cooling, setpoints and dedicated storage (where installed) shift power every day without touching compute. UPS batteries don't count. Layer 2 is the GPUs: tag each workload; protected work is never touched, and shiftable or throttleable work eases only when the grid calls. The mix is an illustrative AI campus.

Why two layers: an hour of GPU compute is worth far more than the ~₹3 per kWh a daily shift saves, so busy GPUs are not idled for bill savings. Saving = daily building flex × 3 h a day × 85% of days × net saving per kWh at the FY30 tariff, less 5% overhead. Model v7 base: 15% of IT load, a team estimate (LBNL lists cooling setpoints and storage as levers); three hours of pre-cooling may need chilled-water storage, and UPS batteries hold only 5-15 minutes, so they are excluded. The pilot tests pre-cooling alone first; any storage capex and running cost is deducted from the customer's saving before Gate G2. Even at 8% daily flex, Case C NPV stays +₹2.1 cr. 25% is the ceiling where spare compute exists. GPU flex (Phoenix field test: 25% for 3 h) earns an earlier connection, not a bill saving. The pilot measures both.

04

A 7 pm grid call: GPUs ease on notice, live AI keeps running

Run the event. The call is scheduled 2-4 hours ahead, so training checkpoints calmly; GPU load steps down, holds, then returns with a small rebound. A 15-minute call is kept only as an emergency option. An independent verifier logs every step: the TTE (Travelling Ticket Examiner), an Independent Verification Agency empanelled by the discom and not involved in programme design or implementation, as the MERC DF & DSM Regulations 2024 provide.

What is real and what is simulated

  • Real: MERC tariff path and time-of-day rules; model v7 formulas; field-test evidence of 25% GPU flexibility for 3 h; published regulator positions (Ofgem, AEMC) and LBNL research.
  • Simulated: the campus, its workload mix, the grid event and the verifier log.
  • Hypotheses to test: the ₹25-40 lakh package price, the 15% daily building flex, the flex-connection route, deal flow of one to two agreements a year.
